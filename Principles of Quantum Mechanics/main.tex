  \section{Hilbert Space}
  \subsection{Definition}
  \begin{definition}
	  (Hilbert space). A Hilbert Space $ \mathcal H $ is a vector space over $ \C $ where
	  \begin{enumerate}
		  \item For $ \phi,\psi \in \mathcal H, a\phi+b\psi \in \mathcal H $ for all $ a,b\in \C $;
		  \item $ \phi + (\psi + \xi) = (\phi + \psi) + \xi $;
		  \item $ \exists 0 \in \mathcal H $ such that $ \psi + 0 = \psi $ for all $ \psi \in \mathcal H $.
	  \end{enumerate}
	  We also have that $ \mathcal H $ is an equipped with an inner product $ (\cdot, \cdot) : \mathcal H \times \mathcal H \to \C $ such that
	  \begin{enumerate}
		  \item (\psi,\phi) = (\phi,\psi);
		  \item $ (\phi, a\psi) = a(\phi,\psi) \forall a\in \C $ but $ (a\phi,\psi) = \overline a (\phi,\psi) $;
		  \item $ (\phi,\phi) \ge 0 $ with equality if and only if $ \phi = 0 $.
	  \end{enumerate}
  \end{definition}
  The inner product gives a \textit{norm} $ ||\psi||= \sqrt{(\psi,\psi)} $. This satisfies the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
  \[
    |(\phi,\psi)| \le ||\psi||||\phi||.
  \]
  $ \mathcal H $ is complete with this norm: a convergent sequence $ \{\phi_n\} $ converges in $ \mathcal H $, so we can take limits and derivatives.
